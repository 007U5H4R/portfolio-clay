# About page redesign (Tushar, 2026-09-29; content complete, lists lightly condensed)

Reference: `about-redesign-reference.jpg`. It is the PRIMARY VISUAL TARGET for composition, hierarchy, density, materiality, rhythm, typography and amount of content. Its FACTS are placeholders: companies (IIT, Google, AIG, HSBC), dates, awards (Nvidia Research Award, Star of the Month, Spot Award), "1 patent filed", "3 research publications" and "2010–2013" must NOT be copied. All facts come from the site's data.

This is a HIGH-EFFORT UI/UX redesign of the existing **About** tab. Do NOT treat the reference as loose inspiration.

## 0. Primary product decision
The current About overlaps too heavily with Experience. Separate their jobs:

- **ABOUT = WHO TUSHAR IS.** It answers:
  - where he started;
  - how his thinking evolved;
  - what connects research, enterprise systems and AI products;
  - what kind of builder or product person he is;
  - what still influences how he works;
  - his values and principles;
  - the intellectual foundation he brings;
  - the problems he likes solving.
- **EXPERIENCE = WHAT TUSHAR DID:** employer-by-employer chronology, titles, dates, responsibilities, achievements, measurable impact, project details and role transitions.
- **CERTIFICATIONS:** formal credentials.
- **EDUCATION:** detailed chronology, if presented within Experience or a dedicated surface.

Do NOT duplicate the full Experience timeline, full education cards or all certification badges inside About. About may reference them narratively and should link to the detailed surfaces.

## 1. Core story
The arc is research → systems → enterprise platforms → product thinking → AI-native products. The through-line is CURIOSITY + SYSTEMS THINKING + USEFUL PRODUCTS.

The feel is "Here is why all these seemingly different chapters belong to the same person", not "Here is my résumé again."

## 2. Approved hero
Eyebrow ABOUT. Title: "A builder who connects deep tech to real-world impact." Supporting copy: "From research labs to production systems, I've always been drawn to solving complex problems and turning them into products people actually use." Keep it concise; no biography paragraph.

## 3. Hero layout
Desktop: LEFT headline + short copy, RIGHT a large editorial collage, split about 44% / 56%. The collage may overlap the paper background. Not a conventional two-column card.

## 4. Hero collage
A custom editorial paper collage:

- research sketches
- notebook pages
- AI/system diagrams
- engineering drawing fragments
- stacked books with spines AI, Systems, Products, Impact
- laptop/product sketches
- city/enterprise imagery
- subtle mountain/horizon
- plants
- pinned/taped paper
- a terracotta circular sun

Every object reinforces research → systems → products → impact. No random decoration.

## 5. Hero annotations
Only 2–3 handwritten annotations, visually secondary. For example: "Research → products → real-world impact.", "Complex ideas. Real people. Bigger impact.", "Different chapters. Same curiosity."

## 6. Materiality
Keep the established language:

- warm cream background with subtle paper grain
- deep navy, terracotta, sage, dusty blue and muted yellow
- natural paper shadows and torn edges
- masking tape, occasional pushpins
- print imperfections

About is more editorial and reflective than Portfolio: less aggressive collage density than the case studies.

## 7. Section 1: Three Chapters
Eyebrow THREE CHAPTERS. Headline "The arc of my journey." Exactly 3 editorial cards: 01 BUILDER · 02 OPERATOR · 03 RESEARCHER (STILL). This is the conceptual backbone.

## 8. Chapter 01 Builder
"Started with machines and research. Learned to go deep, work with ambiguity, and stay curious." Visuals: engineering/machinery sketch, research notebook, industrial or city imagery, technical drawing, subtle research apparatus. Not about one employer.

## 9. Chapter 02 Operator
"Moved into cloud, data and enterprise platforms. Built and shipped at scale with cross-functional teams." Visuals: infrastructure, bridges/systems, architecture diagrams, platform map, cloud/data abstraction, delivery artifacts. Don't list all companies.

## 10. Chapter 03 Researcher (Still)
"Research thinking still shapes how I approach problems — from first principles to real-world constraints." Visuals: research notebook, papers, microscope sketch, publication pages, handwritten annotations, patent/research fragments. Not a detailed education card.

## 11. Chapter card style
A torn editorial card with its own paper tint, one dominant image, a big chapter number, 2–3 sentences max, a subtle rotation, light tape and a natural shadow. Not identical SaaS cards.

## 12. Section 2: Career Across Contexts
Eyebrow CAREER ACROSS CONTEXTS. Headline "Different problems, common thread." NOT another full Experience timeline, but a compact conceptual evolution strip of eras rather than employers: RESEARCH & ENGINEERING → CLOUD & DATA → ENTERPRISE PLATFORMS → AI PRODUCTS. Small company references may appear below the eras; no responsibilities.

## 13–16. Eras
- **Research & Engineering:** Mechanical Engineering + nanotechnology/materials science research. Focus: first principles, experimentation, technical depth, scientific rigour. No degree details.
- **Cloud & Data:** Quantiphi-era cloud/data modernization and adjacent cloud delivery. Focus: systems, data, APIs, cloud modernization, delivery at scale. Company names only if visually useful.
- **Enterprise Platforms:** enterprise-scale product/platform work, including the current Accounts Receivable modernization context. Focus: complex stakeholder systems, modernization, product ownership, enterprise constraints, cross-functional execution. Don't duplicate AmEx achievement bullets.
- **AI Products:** independent AI product building + AI/GenAI work. Focus: ambiguity → prototype, product thinking, AI systems, experimentation, real-user learning. Don't repeat the Portfolio tab.

## 17. Career visual
A compact horizontal pathway with one short descriptor each: Research — First principles · Cloud & Data — Systems at scale · Enterprise — Complex coordination · AI Products — Fast learning loops. No long date ranges unless necessary.

## 18. Career visual language
A thin hand-drawn line, small milestone dots, a subtle icon per phase and minimal text. No second vertical employment timeline: Experience owns that pattern.

## 19. Section 3: Research & Intellectual Work
Eyebrow RESEARCH & INTELLECTUAL WORK. Headline "From labs to lasting ideas." Make the research background visible without becoming an education section. Three compact artifacts:

1. nanotechnology/materials research
2. patent/invention
3. research publications

## 20–22. Research artifacts
- **Nanotechnology:** microscope image, nanoparticle illustration or lab sketch. "Research in materials, sensing and nanoscale systems."
- **Patent:** the portable endotoxin point-of-care device, shown as a patent sheet, technical sketch or device drawing. No excessive legal metadata.
- **Publications:** peer-reviewed papers, shown as a stack of papers, journal cover or article fragments. May reference Langmuir or Soft Matter only if supported by existing data.

## 23. Research rule
Not an Education block (no B.E./M.Tech./university/CGPA/year/coursework). Emphasize "Research shaped how I think."

## 24. Section 4: What Drives Me
Eyebrow WHAT DRIVES ME. Headline "Curiosity, impact and continuous learning." One compact paper card with exactly 3 principles:

- either "Ask better questions. / Build useful products. / Keep learning and sharing."
- or "Stay curious. / Solve useful problems. / Keep raising the bar."

Choose whichever matches the site voice. Not 8–10 values.

## 25. Principle icons
Simple, consistent hand-drawn icons (lightbulb, gear, leaf/growth arrow).

## 26. Optional alternative
Three sticky notes: "GOOD PROBLEMS CREATE GOOD CAREERS." / "PEOPLE MAKE BETTER PRODUCTS THAN PROCESS." / "STAY CURIOUS. KEEP BUILDING." Use this OR the principles card, never both.

## 27. Section 5: Recognition
Eyebrow RECOGNITION. Headline "A few milestones along the way." 2–4 strong items max, using only verified recognition from the data. Possible examples: Google Cloud Partner All-Star — Delivery Excellence, Unsung Hero, Best AI Operator, Buildathon recognition, after verifying the data. NEVER use Nvidia Research Award, Star of the Month, Spot Award or other placeholders.

## 28. Recognition visual
A small icon, the award name and the year. No long description.

## 29. Personality layer
2–3 subtle handwritten annotations across the page, e.g. "Same curiosity. Broader horizons.", "Different hats. Same curiosity.", "Progress is a series of small breakthroughs." No hobbies, trivia or random personal facts unless already part of the story.

## 30–33. Remove / move / separate
- **Remove from About, or move to Experience:** detailed employer history, full job descriptions, long achievement bullets, a duplicated Experience timeline, salary, detailed per-role dates, and detailed work metrics already in Experience.
- **Move the detailed version to Experience** (title, company, dates, achievements, responsibilities, project work). About keeps only a narrative reference.
- **Education:** if detailed education lives in About, move it to Experience if that tab already includes Education. About keeps only research roots, technical foundation and intellectual influence.
- **Certifications:** no badges in About, only a "View certifications →" CTA.

## 34–35. About → Experience CTA
A strong dark-paper strip at the bottom: "Want the full story with roles, achievements and metrics?"

- Primary button: "See full experience →"
- Secondary button: "View certifications →"
- Use the existing routes. No generic "Contact me" here.
- Look: a deep navy torn-paper strip, cream text, a terracotta primary button, a cream secondary button, and a small mountain/horizon illustration at one end. It closes the page.

## 36–38. Typography
Use the existing fonts.

- **Eyebrow:** small uppercase tracked sans, terracotta.
- **Hero:** large high-contrast serif, navy, about 58–70px desktop, line height ~0.98–1.05. Body 18–21px. Not overwhelmingly huge.
- **Section headlines:** 36–46px serif. Card titles 25–32px. Body 16–18px. Meta 13–15px.
- **Handwriting:** annotations only.

## 39–41. Density and rhythm
About 5 major areas:

1. hero
2. Three Chapters
3. Career Across Contexts
4. Research + What Drives Me
5. Recognition + CTA

About 4–5 viewport heights; a recruiter understands it in about 60 seconds. The rhythm alternates: large composition → three cards → thin timeline → two-column section → recognition strip → CTA. Not a 10-section biography.

## 42. Two-column research/values
Desktop: LEFT ~55% Research & Intellectual Work, RIGHT ~45% What Drives Me, with strong asymmetry.

## 43–45. Responsive
- **Tablet:** hero 50/50 or stacked; chapters 3 columns or 2+1; career horizontal if readable, otherwise vertical; research/values stacked.
- **Mobile order:**
  1. hero copy
  2. hero collage
  3. chapters, stacked
  4. career, vertical
  5. research artifacts, scrollable or stacked
  6. values card
  7. recognition
  8. CTA
- **Mobile look:** reduce decorative objects but keep clarity, materiality and typography. Recompose the mobile hero (title, copy, simplified collage); title ~42–48px.

## 46–48. Assets
- **Textures:** no massive raster backgrounds. Use a small optimized repeating texture, CSS, SVG noise or compressed WebP.
- **Dedicated modular About assets**, e.g.:
  - `/public/about/research-sketches.svg`
  - `books-stack.svg`

  - `chapter-builder` / `chapter-operator` / `chapter-researcher`
  - `research-papers.svg`
  - `patent-sheet.svg`
  - `mountains.svg`

  Not one giant screenshot.
- **Avoid:** generic stock or corporate vectors, random AI office scenes, fake logos, fake publication covers.
- **Use:** custom line illustrations, actual logos where appropriate, real artifacts where available, abstract collage otherwise.

## 49–52. Content truth
- Before writing copy, inspect the source data (Experience, Education, Research, Publications, Patent, Awards, Portfolio, Certifications). The reference defines DESIGN, not FACTS.
- Don't invent employment, research, publication, award or education dates.
- Career names may include Godrej Infotech / Godrej & Boyce, Quantiphi, Shellkode, American Express. Don't add unrelated companies from the reference.
- Experience owns WHO/WHERE/WHEN/RESULT; About owns WHY THE JOURNEY MAKES SENSE.

## 53–55. Navigation and links
Keep About active and don't change the other nav items unless fixing a defect. "See full experience →" goes to the existing Experience tab, and "View certifications →" to the existing Certifications tab. No second experience route.

## 56–58. Motion
- **Restrained interactions:** chapter card 1–2px lift, the career line draws on viewport entry, paper notes settle slightly.
- **Scroll reveals, once, respecting prefers-reduced-motion:**
  - hero reveal
  - chapters 80–100ms sequential
  - career thread draws left → right
  - research cards settle
  - recognition fades
- **No:** floating papers, wiggling tape, looping handwriting, parallax on every object, aggressive hover rotation. About feels reflective and composed.

## 59. Accessibility
Meaningful text stays HTML. Informational images get meaningful alt, decorative ones empty alt. Semantic links, WCAG contrast, reduced motion.

## 60–64. Tests of the content
- **Scannability:** the headings alone tell the story: "A builder who connects deep tech to real-world impact." / "The arc of my journey." / "Different problems, common thread." / "From labs to lasting ideas." / "Curiosity, impact and continuous learning." / "A few milestones along the way." If not, rewrite them.
- **Content reduction:** for every block, ask whether it tells WHO Tushar is. If it mainly answers what company, year, metric or project, it belongs in Experience or Portfolio.
- **No duplicates:** no duplicate Experience (role cards, company timelines, exact achievement bullets, responsibilities), no certification gallery, no product cards (a small product-thinking reference is fine).

## 65. Quality target
Editorial, intelligent, personal, warm, technical, reflective, crafted, premium. NOT a corporate résumé, a LinkedIn profile, a bio template, a generic personal site or scrapbook clutter.
